# Panorama concurrentiel — secretaire IA pour artisans

# Concurrents francophones — secrétaires / standards téléphoniques IA pour artisans et TPE (France + Belgique), état au 20 octobre 2026

> Méthode : tous les faits ci-dessous ont été vérifiés sur le site du produit lui-même (page d'accueil, tarifs, politique de confidentialité, sécurité, sous-traitance, mentions légales). Les comparateurs d'affiliation (nerolia-ai.fr, eliocall.com, julsa.fr, lessentieldeleco.fr, proactiveacademy.fr, callbot-ia.com, agent-vocal-ia.fr) et les blogs d'éditeurs (callsens.fr/blog, goodev.fr/blog, aitom.fr/blog) n'ont servi que de pistes pour trouver des noms de produits. Aucun chiffre n'en est repris.

## Q1 — Qui sont les produits réellement sur le marché en français ?

### Takeaway
Le marché francophone des standards téléphoniques IA pour artisans est très fragmenté et très jeune : une douzaine de produits identifiables, presque tous édités par une personne physique ou une micro-structure, aucun acteur dominant, aucun financement public connu. À côté d'eux, deux familles d'acteurs établis : les télésecrétariats humains qui ajoutent de l'IA (IPContact Group) et les opérateurs télécoms qui ajoutent un standard IA à leur PBX (Keyyo / Bouygues Telecom Pro, Mon Standard Pro).

### Inferences
- Le segment « artisan du dépannage » est déjà occupé par au moins 7 produits français nommément positionnés dessus, mais aucun n'a l'allure d'un acteur structuré : pas de pricing page cohérente chez plusieurs, éditeurs personnes physiques, pas de presse.
- Deux stratégies de prix s'affrontent : les pure players IA à 79–349 €/mois, et les opérateurs télécoms qui vendent l'IA comme option d'un forfait à 9 €/mois (Mon Standard Pro). Cette seconde famille est le vrai risque de commoditisation.
- Personne, dans l'échantillon audité, ne fait de l'audio écoutable une promesse centrale — c'est un espace libre (voir Q2 et Q4).

### Gaps
- **monstandard.ai est techniquement inauditable** : le site est rendu en JavaScript et ne renvoie que ses métadonnées à la récupération ; l'accès direct en curl est bloqué (CONNECT tunnel failed, 403) par le proxy. Aucune page tarifs, mentions légales ou politique de confidentialité n'a pu être lue. Toute affirmation sur ses fonctionnalités, son prix ou son éditeur serait une invention.
- Je n'ai pas pu identifier de concurrent français *financé* et *spécifiquement* dédié aux artisans. Les acteurs français financés de la voix IA que je connais (Zaion, Allo-Media, Calldesk/Diabolocom) sont cités comme concurrents par Accueil IA, mais ils visent les grands comptes et les centres de contact, pas les TPE artisanales — je ne les ai pas audités dans ce périmètre.
- Aucune presse startup (Maddyness, FrenchWeb, Les Echos Entrepreneurs) ni Product Hunt n'est ressorti sur aucun de ces noms.

## Q2 — Pour chaque produit : enregistrement des appels, transcription, résumé IA, RDV + agenda, urgence, SMS, app/dashboard

### Takeaway
L'enregistrement audio **écoutable par le pro** est quasi absent du discours commercial : un seul produit (Aitom) l'annonce comme fonctionnalité en page d'accueil, et un seul (Mon Standard Pro) parle d'un « journal d'appels avec enregistrements ». Trois autres (Iksee, AlloBob, Accueil IA, Voicely) ne le vendent pas mais le reconnaissent dans leurs documents juridiques. Tout le reste du marché vend de la **transcription et/ou du résumé**, pas de l'audio.

### Cited Findings
**Aitom** — annonce explicitement enregistrement **et** transcription écrite ; résumé IA et motif de chaque appel ; prise de RDV connectée à l'agenda (Google Calendar n'est pas nommé) ; détection et routage d'urgence avec transfert en temps réel vers la bonne personne ; SMS de confirmation de RDV (montré dans la démo) ; application mobile et desktop avec dashboard, historique d'appels et « Transfert intelligent » ; conservation du numéro existant ou nouveau numéro sur demande — [aitom.fr](https://www.aitom.fr/)

**Iksee** — transcription complète des appels ; fiche de qualification par appel (urgence, client, demande, adresse, créneau) ; résumé SMS + fiche client après chaque appel ; détection d'urgence avec transfert direct vers un mobile pour les vraies urgences ; console web avec historique d'appels et compteur mensuel ; filtrage du démarchage sans notification ; consignes et absences éditables ; multi-destinataires SMS (offre Équipe) ; multi-agences et intégration planning/CRM (offre Réseau, sur devis). **Non mentionnés en page d'accueil** : enregistrement explicite, résumé IA, prise de RDV, Google Calendar, application mobile — [iksee.fr](https://www.iksee.fr/). L'enregistrement est en revanche confirmé dans le contrat de sous-traitance : « enregistrement audio de l'appel » figure comme catégorie de données, et Supabase y est désigné comme stockage des « fiches d'appel, transcriptions et enregistrements » — [iksee.fr/sous-traitance](https://www.iksee.fr/sous-traitance)

**Callsens** — transcription des conversations (légende de capture d'écran) ; rapports détaillés envoyés par e-mail et SMS, et récapitulatif d'activité listant opportunités et rappels ; prise de RDV y compris via Calendly ; **connexion directe à Google Calendar** ; qualification des urgences (mécanisme non décrit) ; SMS pour l'envoi des rapports ; vue de statistiques d'appels en temps réel ; **pas d'application mobile mentionnée** ; **enregistrement des appels : non mentionné** — [callsens.fr](https://callsens.fr/). Intégrations directes Google Calendar et Calendly ; connectables : Stripe, Zapier, Make, HubSpot, Slack, WhatsApp, Gmail ; via webhooks/Zapier : Apimo, Hektor, TheFork, Zenchef, Planity, Treatwell, Genapi — [callsens.fr](https://callsens.fr/)

**Réflex** — modèle différent : rappelle les appels manqués et les demandes de devis du site **sous environ 30 secondes** et qualifie par WhatsApp ou SMS, plutôt que de décrocher ; qualification (type de travaux, marque d'équipement, urgence, adresse ; pour un devis : surface, délai, budget) ; détection d'urgence ; proposition de créneaux selon les disponibilités ; prise de RDV dans **Google Calendar ou Outlook** ; notification SMS avec résumé de la demande (nom, adresse, besoin, urgence) ; devis « quasi pré-rédigé ». **Non mentionnés** : enregistrement, transcription, résumé IA autonome, application ou dashboard — [byreflex.fr](https://byreflex.fr/)

**Accueil IA (GoodEv)** — redirection d'appel depuis l'opérateur existant, le numéro public reste le même ; conversation fluide gérant les interruptions ; prise de RDV sur les disponibilités de l'agenda (**Google Calendar mentionné**) ; confirmation de RDV par SMS ; détection d'urgence avec alerte prioritaire sur le téléphone du dirigeant ; clonage de voix à partir d'un échantillon d'environ 30 secondes ; appels sortants contractuels (rappels de livraison ou de RDV) ; repli configurable quand l'assistant ne sait pas répondre (reformuler, prendre les coordonnées pour rappel, transférer à un humain). **Non mentionnés** : enregistrement, transcription, résumé IA, dashboard — [accueil-ia.fr](https://accueil-ia.fr/callbot-francais/). L'enregistrement est néanmoins confirmé indirectement : la même page indique « enregistrements audio chiffrés en AES-256 » et « purge de l'audio après 30 jours par défaut, configurable » — [accueil-ia.fr](https://accueil-ia.fr/callbot-francais/)

**Assist-iA « Léa »** — prise de RDV dans l'agenda de l'entreprise, avec **Google Calendar, Outlook et Cal.com nommés** ; durées de créneaux paramétrées par type d'intervention ; règles d'urgence (transfert d'appel immédiat ou créneau prioritaire) ; résumé écrit après chaque appel ; détection automatique de langue sur 9 langues ; historique d'appels « consultable à tout moment » via app.assist-ia.io. **Non mentionnés** : enregistrement, transcription, SMS, application dédiée — [assist-ia.io](https://assist-ia.io/receptionniste-ia/artisans)

### Inferences
- Le lecteur audio écoutable depuis le mobile est un différenciateur réel : sur 13 produits audités, **un seul (Aitom) le met en avant commercialement**, et **un seul autre (Mon Standard Pro) l'intègre à un journal d'appels** — mais Mon Standard Pro est un PBX, pas une secrétaire IA, et n'offre ni RDV ni urgence.
- Le combo complet « enregistrement audio écoutable + transcription + résumé + RDV Google Calendar + urgence + SMS + app mobile » n'est revendiqué **par aucun produit audité**. Aitom s'en approche le plus mais ne nomme pas Google Calendar et ne publie aucun prix ; AlloBob s'en approche côté Belgique mais ne parle ni d'audio écoutable ni de transcription.
- La « détection d'urgence » est en revanche quasi universelle (Aitom, Iksee, Callsens, Réflex, Accueil IA, Assist-iA, AlloBob) : ce n'est plus un différenciateur, c'est un prérequis sur ce segment.
- Le SMS de résumé au pro (et non au client) est la convention dominante du segment artisan : Iksee, Callsens, Réflex, AlloBob l'utilisent comme principal canal de restitution. Cela suggère que le dashboard n'est pas le point d'entrée réel de l'artisan.

### Gaps
- Aucune information sur les fonctionnalités de monstandard.ai (site inauditable, cf. Q1).
- Chez Iksee, la page d'accueil ne dit pas si le pro peut **écouter** l'enregistrement : le contrat prouve que l'audio existe et est stocké, mais rien n'indique qu'il soit exposé dans la console web.
- Keyyo : la page de documentation ne dit pas si un enregistrement ou une transcription existe par ailleurs dans l'offre Keyyo/Bouygues Telecom Pro ; seule la fonction IA y est décrite.

## Q3 — Prix réellement publiés

### Takeaway
Le marché publie des prix dans une fourchette resserrée de 79 à 349 €/mois pour un volume de 120 à 1 250 minutes (ou 50 à 300 appels), avec dépassement entre 0,21 et 0,39 €/minute ou 1,50 €/appel. Trois produits ciblant les artisans (monstandard.ai, Aitom, Réflex, plus Assist-iA) refusent de publier un prix et renvoient à un devis après audit — un signe de vente assistée, pas de self-service.

## Q6 — Positionnement et promesse : que prétendent-ils remplacer ?

### Takeaway

- **Callsens** : « Télésecrétariat IA & Permanence Téléphonique 24h/24 pour Artisans » — se positionne directement sur le vocabulaire du télésecrétariat humain qu'il vise à remplacer — [callsens.fr](https://callsens.fr/)
- **Réflex** : « La permanence téléphonique IA des artisans & pros du devis » ; promesse de rappel **sous environ 30 secondes** sur les appels manqués et les demandes de devis du site, avec un devis « quasi pré-rédigé » à la clé — positionné sur la conversion du lead, pas sur l'accueil — [byreflex.fr](https://byreflex.fr/)

- **Assist-iA** : réceptionniste IA « Léa » qui répond 24/7, prend les RDV de dépannage, applique vos règles d'urgence et parle 9 langues — le multilingue comme argument — [assist-ia.io](https://assist-ia.io/receptionniste-ia/artisans)
- **Mon Standard Pro** : standard téléphonique 100 % en ligne, sans installation, les appels sonnent sur le mobile existant ou dans le navigateur ; l'IA est vendue comme **option** d'un forfait télécom, pas comme le produit — [monstandardpro.fr](https://monstandardpro.fr/)
- **AlloBob** : « Bob » répond 24/7 en FR/NL/EN, qualifie les demandes, prend les RDV et envoie un résumé au professionnel ; promesse appuyée sur le trilinguisme belge — [allobob.be](https://allobob.be/)

- **IPContact Group** : « télésecrétariat augmenté par l'IA » — positionnement inverse, l'humain reste au centre et l'IA est un outil ; argumente sur des standardistes « basés exclusivement en France » et sur l'intégration aux logiciels métier du BTP (EBP, Batappli Artisan, Onaya, Organilog) — [ipcontactgroup.com](https://www.ipcontactgroup.com/permanence-telephonique-btp/)

### Inferences
- Trois positionnements distincts coexistent, et ils ne se concurrencent pas vraiment : (1) remplacement 24/7 de la secrétaire (Aitom, Callsens, monstandard.ai, AlloBob) ; (2) couverture des creux et filtrage (Iksee) ; (3) conversion du lead entrant et pré-devis (Réflex). Le troisième est le moins encombré.
- Personne ne vend la **traçabilité** comme bénéfice : aucun produit ne promet « vous pourrez réécouter l'appel et prouver ce qui a été dit ». Pour un artisan en litige sur un devis ou un délai, c'est un bénéfice métier concret et inoccupé.
- L'ancrage « une secrétaire coûte 3 000 €/mois » (Aitom) est le seul chiffre de comparaison trouvé et il est non sourcé sur la page : à utiliser avec prudence.

### Gaps
- Les témoignages et études de cas sont tous auto-hébergés et non vérifiables ; je n'ai trouvé aucune étude de marché indépendante chiffrant le segment français ou belge.

# English-speaking market (US / UK / CA / AU): AI phone receptionists & AI answering services for home-service and trade businesses

*Research conducted 20 October 2026. All prices and feature claims are "as seen" on the dates noted. Where a vendor page does not state something, this is written as "not stated" rather than inferred.*

---

## Key Question 1 — Who are the real players, and what is each one?

### Takeaway
The category splits cleanly into four groups: (a) **AI-native SMB answering services** priced self-serve ($29–$500/mo: Rosie, Goodcall, Smith.ai AI, Jobber Receptionist); (b) **AI-native vertical platforms for home services** sold by demo at $400–$800+/mo (Avoca, Sameday); (c) **field-service-software-native AI agents** bundled into the operating system the trade already uses (ServiceTitan Voice Agents, Housecall Pro CSR AI, Jobber); and (d) **human or human-first answering services** that have bolted AI transcription/summaries on top (Ruby, AnswerConnect, Abby Connect, Smith.ai's human tier). Two of the frequently-listed names are **not in this market at all** — Slang.ai is restaurants-only and Numa is auto dealerships-only.

### Cited Findings

**AI-native, self-serve SMB (the direct analogues of a "Sophie IA")**

- **Rosie** — https://heyrosie.com — "The easiest AI answering service for small business," from $49/month with a 7-day free trial; native iOS and Android apps. Industries listed include home services, construction, plumbing, automotive, landscaping; testimonials from a landscaping company and an electrician — [Rosie homepage](https://heyrosie.com/)
- **Goodcall** — https://www.goodcall.com — "An AI answering service and receptionist for your business," with an agent-builder named Gabi; serves "businesses from small shops to large enterprises, including contact centers"; plans start at $79/month — [Goodcall homepage](https://www.goodcall.com/)
- **Smith.ai** — https://smith.ai — runs **both** an AI receptionist tier and a human receptionist tier, side by side, on separate pricing pages. Positioned as "24/7 AI Receptionists & Live Human Agents" — [Smith.ai AI receptionist pricing](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist); [Smith.ai human pricing](https://smith.ai/pricing)

**AI-native vertical platforms for home services (demo-sold, premium)**

- **Avoca AI** — https://www.avoca.ai — "Always-on agents that answer every call, fill every board, and keep your team focused on the work that matters." Modular: an AI CSR, **Coach** (call scoring), and **Nurture** (SMS/call drip campaigns). Carries a "ServiceTitan Certified App" badge; shows Housecall Pro and FieldRoutes logos — [Avoca homepage](https://www.avoca.ai/)
- **Sameday AI** — https://sameday.ai (the older domain gosameday.com 302-redirects here) — "The AI front office for home services," combining AI agents with CSRs and technicians. Explicitly lists HVAC, plumbing, roofing, solar, pest control, electrician, garage door, landscaping, locksmith, window cleaning and commercial cleaning; claims "nearly 2,000 service businesses" use the platform — [Sameday homepage](https://sameday.ai/)

**Field-service-software-native AI agents (the most dangerous competitive position)**

- **ServiceTitan AI Voice Agents** — https://www.servicetitan.com/features/pro/contact-center/voice-agents — part of Contact Center Pro. Books "straight to the dispatch board with a real-time, unified knowledge of technician availability," using ServiceTitan customer records, addresses, job types and availability. A separate **SMS Agent** handles inbound and outbound texting, "exclusive to ServiceTitan tracking numbers" — [ServiceTitan Voice Agents](https://www.servicetitan.com/features/pro/contact-center/voice-agents)

Housecall Pro's own navigation also lists "AI Team," "Call Answering (HCP Assist)," "Answering Service Software" and "Voice Solution" as separate items — [Housecall Pro CSR AI](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/csr-ai/); [Housecall Pro homepage](https://www.housecallpro.com/)
- **Jobber AI Receptionist** — https://www.getjobber.com/features/ai-receptionist/ — handles calls *and* texts, books visits using the business's existing online booking settings, and texts back callers who hang up — [Jobber AI Receptionist](https://www.getjobber.com/features/ai-receptionist/)

**Human / human-first answering services with AI added on**

- **Ruby** (Ruby Receptionists) — https://www.ruby.com — explicitly human: "100% live, personalized communication." AI is positioned as "optional AI enhancements" included at no extra cost, including "AI-assistant-supported call flows" — [Ruby pricing](https://www.ruby.com/pricing/)
- **AnswerConnect** — https://www.answerconnect.com — human-staffed, and markets *against* AI: "Win more business with real people, not automated voices" and "No voicemail, no bots." Offers appointment scheduling with integrations such as Setmore — [AnswerConnect homepage](https://www.answerconnect.com/)
- **Abby Connect** — https://www.abby.com — hybrid, human-first: "Every Abby customer talks to a real, live, trained receptionist" and "Our service is fundamentally human." AI sits on top as "Abby Intelligence," combining "Abby Human Intelligence and Abby Artificial Intelligence" — [Abby Connect blog](https://www.abby.com/blog/abby-ai-transcripts-summaries/)

**Named in the brief but NOT in this market**

### Inferences
- For a France/Belgium product, the realistic competitive reference set is **Rosie, Goodcall, Smith.ai, Avoca, Sameday, and the FSM-native agents (ServiceTitan / Jobber / Housecall Pro)**. Slang.ai and Numa should be dropped from any competitive matrix — including them would mislead.
- The FSM-native agents are the structural threat: they win on booking quality because they already own the dispatch board and customer record. A standalone product must either integrate deeply with the local equivalent or win on something the FSM vendors treat as secondary (which, per Q2 below, is the call *evidence* layer).

## Key Question 2 — Is call recording + transcription + AI summary a STANDARD advertised feature, or rare?

### Takeaway
**It is standard and advertised as table stakes at the SMB AI-native end of the market — but it is NOT uniformly the headline feature, and the "original audio playback" piece is noticeably weaker or absent at the premium/vertical end.** Recording + transcript + summary is explicitly bundled into *every* plan, including free tiers, at Rosie, Smith.ai and Jobber; Goodcall advertises recording + transcript but not summaries; Avoca and Housecall Pro CSR AI advertise summaries/scoring while saying nothing about giving the owner the raw audio.

### Cited Findings

**Recording + transcript + summary explicitly bundled in all plans (including the cheapest)**

- **Rosie**: "Every call summarized, transcribed, and recorded." The line item "Call summaries, transcripts & recordings" appears on **all plans**, starting at the $49/mo Professional tier — [Rosie homepage](https://heyrosie.com/); [Rosie pricing](https://heyrosie.com/pricing)
- **Smith.ai (AI tier)**: "Call recording, transcription, and AI summaries" are **included in the Free ($0/mo) plan and all higher plans** — [Smith.ai AI receptionist pricing](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist)
- **Jobber**: "Track every call and text with a short summary, full transcript, and recording." Each conversation is logged — [Jobber AI Receptionist](https://www.getjobber.com/features/ai-receptionist/)
- **Sameday AI**: calls include **audio playback with speed controls**, a "View transcript" option, and AI-generated summaries *and classifications* — [Sameday homepage](https://sameday.ai/)

**Recording + playback + transcript advertised, but no summary**

- **Goodcall**: "Every call recorded and transcribed," and — unusually explicit about the playback UX — "**Play back any call and read the transcript, word for word.**" AI summaries are **not mentioned** on the homepage; the pricing page does not mention recording, transcripts or summaries at all, only "Call history" retention windows (30 days / 90 days / Unlimited by tier) — [Goodcall homepage](https://www.goodcall.com/); [Goodcall pricing](https://www.goodcall.com/pricing)

**Summary / scoring advertised, raw audio NOT advertised on the marketing page**

- **Avoca AI**: the homepage mentions **neither** recording, playback, transcripts, **nor** summaries. Its Coach product says "Score every call, surface missed opportunities, and give managers real-time visibility" — [Avoca homepage](https://www.avoca.ai/)
- **Avoca Coach page**: confirms scoring ("Every call scored against your company's rubric") and summaries, but "does not mention call recordings, listening to calls, or transcripts" — [Avoca Coach](https://avoca.ai/coach)
- **Avoca's ServiceTitan marketplace listing** frames recordings as the thing you *avoid*: managers can review performance "without listening to hours of recordings." Transcription and summaries are not described — [ServiceTitan Marketplace: Avoca AI](https://marketplace.servicetitan.com/partner/avoca-ai)
- **Avoca's own help docs, however, do confirm the capability exists** once you are a customer: a call's details "can include an audio recording and transcript," and the docs advise "Listen to the recording when exact wording, timing, audio quality, or transfer behavior matters," plus the caution that "A summary is useful for orientation but is **not a substitute for the source call**." Additional per-call details listed: disposition, call classification, booking/bookability outcome, customer and call metadata, and "evaluations" when enabled. Recording **export** "may be limited to authorized users and supported calls" — [Avoca help: reviewing calls in the dashboard](https://help.avoca.ai/How-to-review-calls-in-the-Avoca-Dashboard-2f0f2b56d4d5802fb74bf5b5c7140f6d.md)
- **Housecall Pro CSR AI**: "You'll get call summaries, booking details, and customer info logged in your call log and calendar" (web and mobile). **Call recordings: not mentioned. Transcripts: not mentioned.** — [Housecall Pro CSR AI](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/csr-ai/)

**Full stack advertised by the incumbent FSM vendor**

- **ServiceTitan Voice Agents**: all three layers advertised. You can "review call recordings, booking outcomes, and performance metrics," and "**Access AI-generated summaries and transcripts for every call and every job booked**" — [ServiceTitan Voice Agents](https://www.servicetitan.com/features/pro/contact-center/voice-agents)

**Human-first services: recording is an add-on or absent, transcripts/summaries are the recent AI retrofit**

- **Smith.ai human tier**: "Call recording & transcription" is a **paid add-on at $0.25 per call**, which "includes access to recordings and searchable transcripts." AI summaries are not mentioned on the human pricing page — [Smith.ai pricing](https://smith.ai/pricing)
- **Ruby**: lists "AI-powered transcripts and sentiment analysis," "Voicemail transcription" and "Chat transcripts." **Call recording: not mentioned. AI summaries: not mentioned** — [Ruby pricing](https://www.ruby.com/pricing/)
- **Abby Connect**: launched "AI Call Transcripts" and "AI Call Summaries," both in the AbbyGo app and Abby Portal. "Call Transcripts gives you an automatic play-by-play of every conversation for easy recording and reference"; "AI Call Summaries give you an at-a-glance view of each call's events," including caller information, action items and conclusions, with app or email notification when a call has been summarised. Tellingly, the selling line is "**Skip listening to call recordings**" — [Abby Connect](https://www.abby.com/blog/abby-ai-transcripts-summaries/)
- **AnswerConnect**: call recording **not mentioned**, transcripts **not mentioned**, AI summaries **not mentioned** — [AnswerConnect homepage](https://www.answerconnect.com/)

### Inferences
- **The feature bar is clear: for a self-serve AI receptionist aimed at trades, recording + transcript + AI summary is the price of entry, not a differentiator.** A product shipping without all three in its cheapest paid plan would be visibly behind Rosie at $49/mo and Smith.ai at $0/mo.
- There is a real split in *philosophy* about the audio. Rosie, Goodcall, Sameday and ServiceTitan treat the recording as something the owner wants. Abby ("Skip listening to call recordings") and Avoca's marketplace copy ("without listening to hours of recordings") treat the recording as a **cost** the AI removes. Avoca's internal docs, however, side with the first camp ("not a substitute for the source call"), which suggests the anti-recording framing is marketing positioning rather than a product belief.
- **Structured field extraction** (the Sophie IA spec's `intent / service / urgency / customer_name / address / requested_date`) is advertised much more thinly than recording/transcript/summary. The nearest things found were Sameday's "AI-generated summaries **and classifications**" and Avoca's disposition / call classification / bookability outcome. Nobody found advertises a rich extracted-fields panel as a headline. **This is a genuine gap to attack.**

### Gaps
- Retention periods for audio are almost never published. Rosie's FAQ literally poses the question "Are calls recorded? How long are recordings stored?" and the answer was not rendered in the fetched page. Goodcall publishes "Call history" windows (30d/90d/unlimited) but does not say whether that governs *audio* specifically.
- Whether Housecall Pro CSR AI actually records and simply doesn't advertise it is unknown — recorded as "not stated," not as "does not record."

## Key Question 5 — Legal handling of recording: consent, two-party-consent states, notices, retention, DPA

### Takeaway
**This is the weakest, least-developed area across the whole category — and therefore the clearest opening for a GDPR-native European product.** Only Rosie was found to publish a substantive, quotable consent position, and it works by **pushing the legal burden entirely onto the business customer**. No player found publishes two-party-consent state guidance, a retention period, or a DPA on a page reachable from their marketing site.

### Cited Findings

**Rosie — the only detailed published position found, and it is a liability-shifting one**

- "Calls with AI Receptionists are recorded for quality assurance, to improve the Services" (plus internal business purposes) — [Rosie privacy policy](https://heyrosie.com/legal/privacy)
- The recording notice is **optional and configured by the business**, not by Rosie: "**The Subscriber may set up the AI Receptionist to notify you that a call is being recorded**" — [Rosie privacy policy](https://heyrosie.com/legal/privacy)
- Consent is treated as implied by continuing: "**By staying on the line after receiving the notification, you consent to the call recording.**" And: "If you do not consent to call recording, you may end the call or ask not to be recorded." — [Rosie privacy policy](https://heyrosie.com/legal/privacy)
- The burden is explicitly the customer's: "**The Subscriber is solely responsible for providing Callers with notice and obtaining their consent.**" Separately: "Callers consent to this processing by submitting their Personal Information to the AI Receptionist." — [Rosie privacy policy](https://heyrosie.com/legal/privacy)
- Ownership and retention are also delegated: "**Call recordings, transcripts, and other data from calls belong to the Subscriber, not Rosie.**" "**Each Subscriber determines how data is used and how long it is retained.**" "Rosie retains Personal Information about Callers until the Subscriber deletes it or instructs us to do so." The only fixed period published: "**We delete Subscriber account data 30 days after the Subscriber's subscription is cancelled.**" — [Rosie privacy policy](https://heyrosie.com/legal/privacy)
- On vendors: "Our service providers are subject to contractual agreements that protect your Personal Information" — no sub-processors named — [Rosie privacy policy](https://heyrosie.com/legal/privacy)
- **Rosie does not mention two-party consent states, GDPR, EU users, a DPA, or named sub-processors**, and geographically restricts itself: the Services are offered in the United States and Canada, and "**Callers and others outside the United States or Canada should not use the Services**" — [Rosie privacy policy](https://heyrosie.com/legal/privacy)

**Avoca — delegates to the customer's own policies, publishes no periods**

- The call-review help page "has no consent guidance and no retention periods." On exports it says only: "**Treat exported call data according to your organization's privacy and retention policies.**" Recording export "may be limited to authorized users and supported calls," and readers are told to check with "your Avoca administrator or your organization's policy owners" — [Avoca help docs](https://help.avoca.ai/How-to-review-calls-in-the-Avoca-Dashboard-2f0f2b56d4d5802fb74bf5b5c7140f6d.md)

**Abby Connect — nothing substantive**

- Mentions call recordings only in passing ("Skip listening to call recordings"). "It says nothing about consent, whether callers are told a call is recorded or transcribed, or how long recordings are kept." It refers to protecting "your and your caller's data" without explaining what that involves — [Abby Connect](https://www.abby.com/blog/abby-ai-transcripts-summaries/)

**Retention as a product setting rather than a legal statement**

- Goodcall is the only vendor found that puts a retention-like figure on the pricing page, and does so as a feature tier rather than a policy: "Call history" of **30 days** (Starter), **90 days** (Growth), **Unlimited** (Scale) — [Goodcall pricing](https://www.goodcall.com/pricing)
- Rosie's own FAQ poses "Are calls recorded? How long are recordings stored?" — the answer was not rendered in the fetched page, i.e. not available to a prospect reading the pricing page — [Rosie pricing](https://heyrosie.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- The industry norm in the US is **implied consent by staying on the line, with the notice itself made optional and configurable, and the compliance obligation contractually transferred to the trade business.** That is tenable in a one-party-consent US state. It is **not** a design that survives GDPR / the Belgian and French regimes, where recording a customer call requires a lawful basis, genuine information, and a defined, limited retention period.
- Therefore: an EU-built product cannot copy this architecture. But the flip side is that **no established English-speaking player has built the GDPR-native version**, so there is no incumbent playbook to compete against on that axis — consent capture, per-organisation retention policy, caller notice as a *default-on* behaviour, and a real DPA are all differentiators rather than catch-up work. This aligns with the Sophie IA spec's §42 instruction not to hard-code a universal legal assumption.
- Rosie's "data belongs to the Subscriber, each Subscriber determines retention" model is actually a reasonable **technical** pattern to reuse (per-organisation configurable retention, per §13 and §40 of the Sophie IA spec) — it just has to be paired with EU-appropriate defaults and an actual DPA, rather than used to disclaim responsibility.

### Gaps
- **Smith.ai's privacy policy could not be read.** Both `smith.ai/privacy-policy` and `smith.ai/privacy` failed to return policy text (the second returned only a meta description and a contact form). Smith.ai sells call recording as a $0.25/call add-on, so it certainly has a position — but it was **not obtained** and must not be characterised.
- No DPA, sub-processor list, or SOC 2 / ISO 27001 claim was located for any player in this set. Absence of evidence here is partly a search-depth limitation, not proof that none exist.
- No vendor was found publishing two-party-consent-state guidance. The search surfaced only third-party SEO content on that topic (e.g. myaifrontdesk, thoughtly, retellai blog posts), which per the brief's constraints is not usable as a factual source about any vendor's practice.

## Key Question 8 — Is "listen to the original recording" a differentiator or a commodity?

### Takeaway
**It is a commodity capability but an under-exploited pitch.** Every serious AI-native player can produce the audio, yet almost nobody leads with it — and two players actively pitch *not* listening. There is no player in this market whose core positioning is "listen to the original recording of the call."

### Cited Findings
- The clearest pro-audio pitch found is **Goodcall's**: "Every call recorded and transcribed… **Play back any call and read the transcript, word for word**" — but it sits as one bullet among several, not as the product's identity — [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/)
- **Sameday** gives the most developed audio *UX* found — "audio playback with speed controls" alongside a "View transcript" option and AI summaries with classifications — again as a dashboard capability, not a positioning claim — [Sameday](https://sameday.ai/)
- **Rosie** states "Every call summarized, transcribed, and recorded" as a single compound claim; the recording is the third item in the list, and the playback experience is not described at all — [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/)
- **Two players pitch the opposite.** Abby Connect sells its AI summaries as a way to "**Skip listening to call recordings**" — [Abby Connect](https://www.abby.com/blog/abby-ai-transcripts-summaries/). Avoca's ServiceTitan marketplace listing says managers can review performance "**without listening to hours of recordings**" — [ServiceTitan Marketplace](https://marketplace.servicetitan.com/partner/avoca-ai)
- **Yet Avoca's own documentation contradicts that framing** and makes the strongest statement found anywhere in this research about *why* the raw audio matters: "**Listen to the recording when exact wording, timing, audio quality, or transfer behavior matters**," and "A summary is useful for orientation but is **not a substitute for the source call**" — [Avoca help docs](https://help.avoca.ai/How-to-review-calls-in-the-Avoca-Dashboard-2f0f2b56d4d5802fb74bf5b5c7140f6d.md)
- **ServiceTitan** bundles all three without hierarchy: "review call recordings, booking outcomes, and performance metrics" and "Access AI-generated summaries and transcripts for every call and every job booked" — [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/features/pro/contact-center/voice-agents)
- **Where audio is genuinely weak or absent from the marketing**: Housecall Pro CSR AI (summaries yes, recordings and transcripts **not mentioned**) — [Housecall Pro CSR AI](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/csr-ai/); Ruby (transcripts yes, **call recording not mentioned**) — [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/pricing/); AnswerConnect (**none mentioned**) — [AnswerConnect](https://www.answerconnect.com/); Avoca's and Numa's own marketing pages — [Avoca](https://www.avoca.ai/); [Numa](https://www.numa.com/)
- **Smith.ai's human tier prices the audio explicitly** at $0.25/call for "access to recordings and searchable transcripts," which is the only place in this market where a monetary value is attached to the recording layer — [Smith.ai pricing](https://smith.ai/pricing)

### Inferences
- **Direct answer for the Sophie IA brief: audio playback is a commodity you must have, not a feature you can win on by having.** Treating it as a V1 requirement (Sophie IA spec §13, §31) is correct and matches the market — a product without it would look deficient next to Rosie at $49/mo.
- **But the *pitch* is available, because the market is split and confused about it.** Abby and Avoca's marketing both tell the owner the AI means they never have to listen — while Avoca's own docs tell its users the summary is "not a substitute for the source call." That contradiction is exactly the trust gap an artisan feels: an AI took a call about their business and they have only the AI's word for what was said. A positioning built on *verifiability* — "the AI's summary, and the proof, one tap apart" — is unoccupied in this market.
- The genuinely under-served layer is not the audio and not the summary but **structured extraction with timestamp-linked transcript navigation**. Nobody found advertises clicking a transcript passage to seek the audio (the Sophie IA spec §14 anticipates this), and only Sameday and Avoca advertise any form of classification. That is where a new entrant can be visibly better rather than merely at parity.
- Retention/consent is the second unoccupied layer in Europe (see Q5), and it pairs naturally with the verifiability pitch: in FR/BE, "you can hear the original call" must be delivered alongside "and here is exactly how long we keep it and what the caller was told."

### Gaps
- Could not determine whether Housecall Pro CSR AI records at all, or simply does not advertise it; recorded as "not stated."
- No pricing was found anywhere in the market for recording/playback as a *separately charged* AI feature — Smith.ai's $0.25/call applies to its human service — so there is no benchmark for monetising the audio layer independently.
